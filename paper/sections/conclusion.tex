\section{Conclusion}

Across three studies totaling over 7,000 scored trials, we find a consistent pattern: models cannot distinguish between revision requests that reflect genuine quality gaps and those that do not.

Study~1 (3,840 trials) establishes that the decision to revise is controlled by follow-up prompt phrasing, not the user's stated quality threshold. Models possess threshold-calibration ability within triggered revisions (Spearman $\rho = -0.14$ to $-0.50$), but this ability is locked behind a phrasing-sensitive gate that fires regardless of user preferences.

Study~2 (1,728 trials) shows that the gate is modulated by conversational history, with sharp model divergence: GPT-4o's revision rate jumps from 31\% to 98\% with a single prior round, while Claude and Gemini resist. Reverse momentum confirms the gate tracks the most recent conversational signal in both directions.

Study~3 (720 trials, 6 models, 5 turns) demonstrates the downstream cost: repeated balanced revision opportunities degrade output quality by 1.23 levels on a cohort-locked panel, with five of six models showing deterioration. Models revise past sufficiency 64.3\% of the time, yet prefer their first draft 83.7\% of the time when freed from conversational pressure. The quality problem is not revision capacity: targeted feedback outperforms generic probing by 2.0 levels, showing that models revise effectively when given a quality signal and poorly when asked to revise without one.

Together, these results locate a specific alignment failure: models possess the ability to assess quality (Study~3, self-reflection and reversibility), to calibrate revision intensity to user preferences (Study~1, Stage~2), and to revise effectively when directed (Study~3, targeted feedback). What they lack is the ability to integrate these capabilities at the revision gate, where the decision to revise is dominated by conversational pragmatics rather than quality assessment. Future work should test whether targeted interventions (quality-gated revision routing, threshold injection, directed feedback) can close this gap.
