\section{Conclusion}

Given repeated undirected revision opportunities, most models decline to genuinely revise (75\% of post-Turn~1 outputs are meta-responses), and those that do revise drift. Sufficient work is not made incomplete but over-elaborated, the share rated Overdone rising from 4\% to 40\% ($p = 4.0 \times 10^{-5}$), a fall of $-0.38$ ordinal levels. It is costly: 62.1\% of output tokens are spent past Turn~1, the optimal stopping point for all six.

More turns do not help; naming the fault does. Told what to fix, a model produces revisions 1.01 levels higher than generic iteration ($p = 1.9 \times 10^{-19}$). Systems should evaluate before revising, because intrinsic self-correction is not enough: it requires extrinsic direction, and revision robustness deserves evaluation alongside single-turn capability.
