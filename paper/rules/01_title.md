# Rules: Title

Retrieved 2026-09-02. 54 papers. Every rule names the papers supporting it.

**Provenance.** Every title below was fetched from its arXiv abstract page or from an
arXiv advanced-search results page on 2026-09-02 and transcribed verbatim. No title in
this file was written from memory. The venue column reports the author-supplied arXiv
Comments field as it stood on 2026-09-02; it was not independently checked against
proceedings, so a paper marked "preprint (no venue in comments)" may well be published
without saying so on arXiv. Two known cases of this are flagged in the corpus notes.
Direct network access was blocked in this environment, so all retrieval ran through the
fetch tool against arxiv.org; the ACL Anthology was not queried directly, and no
Anthology IDs are recorded here.

**What is not covered.** The corpus is arXiv-indexed computer science only. Work
published at CHI, CSCW or TACL that never posted a preprint is absent, which biases the
sample toward NLP and away from HCI. Non-English titles were not searched. Citation
counts and acceptance status beyond the Comments field were not checked.

## What the field does

### Title form

Of 54 titles: **32 colon two-part**, **9 plain declarative sentences**, **9 noun
phrases**, **4 question form**. The colon is the default and everything else is a
deviation from it.

The colon share rises over the sample: 6 of 15 in 2023 (40 percent), 14 of 22 in 2024
(64 percent), 10 of 13 in 2025 (77 percent), 2 of 4 in 2026 (the 2026 cell has only 4
observations and should not be read as a reversal). The 2023 titles that are not
colon-form are concentrated in the earliest, most foundational work: *Judging
LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena* (arXiv:2306.05685), *Towards
Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models* (arXiv:2310.13548), *Large Language Models
Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet* (arXiv:2310.01798). By 2025 the plain forms survive
mainly where the finding is a single sentence: *LLMs Get Lost In Multi-Turn
Conversation* (arXiv:2505.06120), *Interaction Context Often Increases Sycophancy in
LLMs* (arXiv:2509.12517).

In the 32 colon titles the head before the colon is short: median 3 words, range 1 to 8,
and 19 of 32 have a head of 3 words or fewer. The head is either a coined system name
(*CRITIC*, arXiv:2305.11738; *ELEPHANT*, arXiv:2505.13995; *InfoQuest*,
arXiv:2502.12257; *Self-Polish*, arXiv:2305.14497; *Self-Refine*, arXiv:2303.17651) or a
short claim (*Confidence Matters*, arXiv:2402.12563; *Reasoning Isn't Enough*,
arXiv:2506.21561; *Clarification Is Not Enough*, arXiv:2605.25204; *Feedback Friction*,
arXiv:2506.11930). The tail after the colon then states the method or the scope.

### Word length

Median 9.5 words, mean 10.0, range 4 to 18. Distribution: 4 to 6 words, 5 titles; 7 to
9, 22; 10 to 12, 15; 13 to 15, 9; 16 to 18, 3. Seventeen titles are 8 words or fewer;
twelve are 13 or more.

Length varies by form. Noun phrases are shortest (median 8, range 4 to 14). Declaratives
are next (median 9, range 6 to 13). Colon titles are longest (median 10, range 5 to 18),
because the colon buys a second clause. The shortest title in the corpus is
*Self-Preference Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge* (arXiv:2410.21819, 4 words); the longest is
*An Empirical Study of LLM-as-a-Judge for LLM Evaluation: Fine-tuned Judge Model is not
a General Substitute for GPT-4* (arXiv:2403.02839, 18 words).

### Full declarative sentences and their verbs

Nine titles are a complete sentence with a finite verb and no colon. The verbs are:

|---|---|---|

| LLMs cannot find reasoning errors, but can correct them given the error location | arXiv:2311.08516 | cannot / can (contrastive modal pair) |
| LLMs Get Lost In Multi-Turn Conversation | arXiv:2505.06120 | get lost |

Six of the nine use a modal or a copula (*cannot*, *can*, *is not*, *have*). Three use a
transitive lexical verb with a causal reading (*causes*, *reduces*, *increases*). Only
one, *get lost*, is figurative, and it is the title that has been imitated most:
arXiv:2602.07338 reuses the whole phrase and adds a cause in front of it.

A verb naming a requirement or a dependency (*requires*, *needs*, *depends on*) does not
appear in any of the 54. It is used in the wider LLM literature, and four verified
examples establish that the construction is intelligible to this audience: *Automated
Annotation with Generative AI Requires Validation* (arXiv:2306.00176), *Banishing LLM
Hallucinations Requires Rethinking Generalization* (arXiv:2406.17642), *Decoding with
Limited Teacher Supervision Requires Understanding When to Trust the Teacher*
(arXiv:2406.18002, EMNLP 2024), *Social Bias Evaluation for Large Language Models
Requires Prompt Variations* (arXiv:2407.03129). All four are declarative, no colon, and
they run 5 to 12 words.

### Negative-result titles

Twenty of the 54 report a limitation as the finding. The constructions, counted by
feature (a title can use more than one):

- **Explicit negation, 8 titles.** *Cannot* (arXiv:2310.01798, arXiv:2311.08516, and
  *What They Can and Cannot Do*, arXiv:2409.11239), *Doesn't* (arXiv:2310.12397),
  *Isn't* (arXiv:2506.21561), *is Not* (arXiv:2410.20513, arXiv:2605.25204), *is not*
  (arXiv:2403.02839). The negation is placed on the model or the method, never on the
  reader and never on prior work.
- **Deficit noun, 7 titles.** *Limitations* (arXiv:2402.08115), *Failure*
  (arXiv:2502.15851), *failures* (arXiv:2510.18254), *Dark Side* (arXiv:2412.14959),
  *Bottleneck* (arXiv:2605.25204), *Friction* (arXiv:2506.11930), *Strengths and
  Limitations* (arXiv:2506.06941).
- **Failure verb, 4 titles.** *Get Lost* (arXiv:2505.06120, arXiv:2602.07338),
  *Struggle* (arXiv:2404.04298, arXiv:2506.11930).
- **Illusion frame, 3 titles in corpus.** arXiv:2510.18254, arXiv:2506.06941,
  arXiv:2502.15851.
- **Question with a doubt adverb, 2 titles.** *Really* (arXiv:2310.08118), *Actually*
  (arXiv:2406.01297).

By form, the 20 negative titles split 10 colon, 6 declarative, 2 noun phrase, 2
question. Declaratives are over-represented among negatives: 6 of 9 declaratives in the
whole corpus report a limitation, against 10 of 32 colon titles. A flat sentence is the
form this field reaches for when the finding is that something does not work.

The negation is stated at its measured strength, not softened and not inflated. *Large
Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet* adds *Yet*, which restricts the claim
to current models rather than to the architecture. *LLMs cannot find reasoning errors,
but can correct them given the error location* puts the positive half in the same
sentence, so the title is a result rather than a complaint. *Clarification Is Not
Enough* names what remains after the negation instead of stopping at it.

### Named phenomena and their reappearance

Titles in this field routinely coin a phrase and then use it. Six abstracts were fetched
and the title phrase counted in each:

| Title phrase | ID | Occurrences in abstract |
|---|---|---|
| Self-Refine | arXiv:2303.17651 | 4 |
| social sycophancy | arXiv:2505.13995 | 4 (and ELEPHANT twice) |
| discriminat- (from *Discriminating*) | arXiv:2404.04298 | 4 |
| self-correct- | arXiv:2310.01798 | 4 (with *intrinsic self-correction* once) |
| multi-turn | arXiv:2505.06120 | 3 (*lost* once, in the closing sentence) |
| underspecified | arXiv:2411.07237 | 2 (*contextualized evaluations* once) |

In all six the title's operative phrase reappears in the abstract, most often four times.
The one-occurrence cases are instructive: *lost* appears once in arXiv:2505.06120, in the
final sentence, where the abstract cashes out the metaphor ("when LLMs take a wrong turn
in a conversation, they get lost and do not recover"). A figurative title term is paid
for by a literal definition somewhere in the abstract. Only 6 of 54 abstracts were
checked, so this is a pattern in a small sample, not a rate for the corpus.

### Hedges

Counted across all 54 titles: *Can* 5, *Cannot* 2, *Not* 2, *When* 2, *Yet* 1,
*Towards* 1, *Really* 1, *Actually* 1, *Often* 1, *Does* 1.

Each does distinct work:

- **Yet** (arXiv:2310.01798) bounds the claim in time. It concedes that future models may
  differ while asserting that present ones do not, and it is the reason that title
  survived two years of contradiction without being wrong.
- **Can / Cannot** state a capability claim rather than a performance number. Both
  polarities appear, sometimes on the same object: arXiv:2405.14092 (*Can Self-Correct
  with Key Condition Verification*) against arXiv:2310.01798.
- **When** (arXiv:2406.01297, arXiv:2606.23196) converts a disputed yes-or-no into a
  conditions question, and both instances are survey or analysis papers rather than
  primary experiments.
- **Really / Actually** signal that the paper is auditing a claim someone else made
  (arXiv:2310.08118, arXiv:2406.01297).
- **Often** (arXiv:2509.12517) states frequency rather than universality, which is what
  a behavioural measurement across conditions can support.
- **Towards** (arXiv:2310.13548) marks incompleteness. It appears once in 54 titles, and
  the one instance is a 2023 paper.

### Trend, 2023 to 2026

Three movements are visible in the corpus and in the search counts behind it.

1. **The colon share rises** (40 percent in 2023 to 77 percent in 2025, above).
2. **"Intrinsic self-correction" hardened into a term of art.** The phrase appears in 7
   arXiv cs titles and 24 arXiv cs records overall, all dated 2024 or later, beginning
   with arXiv:2402.12563 (February 2024) and running through arXiv:2606.23196 (June
   2026). Its first use in the corpus is definitional rather than titular: the abstract
   of arXiv:2310.01798 (October 2023) introduces it as "the notion of intrinsic
   self-correction, whereby an LLM attempts to correct its initial responses based
   solely on its inherent capabilities, without the crutch of external feedback."
3. **The "Illusion of X" frame grew fast and is now crowded.** Counting arXiv cs titles
   containing *illusion*: 12 in 2023, 41 in 2024, 71 in 2025, 128 in the first eight
   months of 2026. A construction at that density no longer distinguishes a paper.

Note on the paired term: *extrinsic* is not the established opposite of *intrinsic* in
this literature. The exact phrase "extrinsic self-correction" returns one arXiv cs record
(arXiv:2410.20513). The phrase the field uses is *external feedback*: it is the wording
in arXiv:2310.01798's abstract, in arXiv:2506.11930's title (*Feedback Friction: LLMs
Struggle to Fully Incorporate External Feedback*), and in arXiv:2410.20513's framing.

## Rules

**R1. Aim for 6 to 12 words.** The corpus median is 9.5 and 39 of 54 titles fall in 6 to
12. Below 6 the title cannot name both the object and the finding; above 13 it is almost
always because a colon tail is enumerating scope (arXiv:2403.02839, 18 words;
arXiv:2506.06941, 18 words). Checkable: count the words.

**R2. Use a colon only if the head is a coined name or a short claim, and keep the head
to 3 words or fewer.** Nineteen of the 32 colon titles keep the head to 3 words or fewer. A colon head that is a
noun-phrase description of the paper rather than a name or a claim is the weak form of
the construction. Checkable: count the words before the colon and classify the head.

**R3. If the finding is a limitation, a flat declarative is a field-standard choice, not
an eccentric one.** Six of the 9 declaratives in the corpus report a limitation, and the
best-known negative results in the area are declaratives without colons
(arXiv:2310.01798, arXiv:2311.08516, arXiv:2505.06120). Checkable: the title parses as
one sentence with a finite verb and no colon.

**R4. State the negation at its measured strength and bound it.** *Cannot ... Yet*
(arXiv:2310.01798) bounds by time. *cannot X, but can Y given Z* (arXiv:2311.08516)
bounds by condition and names the condition. *Often* (arXiv:2509.12517) bounds by
frequency. A negation with no bound claims more than a 720-trial study measures.
Checkable: identify the qualifier and say what it excludes.

**R5. Name the phenomenon in the title only if the abstract will define it.** Every one
of the six abstracts checked reuses the title's operative phrase, most of them four
times, and the single-use case (arXiv:2505.06120) uses it to define the metaphor
outright. Checkable: grep the abstract for the title's operative phrase; it should appear
at least once as a definition and preferably more.

**R6. Use the field's existing vocabulary for the concept the paper shares with prior
work, and coin only for the concept that is new.** *Intrinsic self-correction* is
established (7 titles, 24 records, all post-2023). *Sycophancy*, *multi-turn*,
*underspecified*, *external feedback* are likewise in circulation. A title that coins
where the field already has a word makes the paper harder to find; a title that reuses
where the paper is genuinely new gives away the contribution.

**R7. Do not put a hedge in the title that the design cannot support, and do not omit one
it needs.** *Towards* appears once in 54 titles and in a 2023 paper; it reads as
incompleteness and should be avoided for a finished result. *Yet*, *When*, *Often* and
the modal *can / cannot* each state a specific bound and are used freely.

**R8. Put the object of study before the finding.** Every declarative in the corpus leads
with the thing being measured (*Large Language Models*, *LLMs*, *Self-correction*,
*Interaction Context*, *Intent Mismatch*, *Simple synthetic data*) and closes with what
happens to it. This is what makes the title searchable on its subject.

**R9. Check the title against the field's stock of constructions before adopting it.**
The *Illusion of X* frame went from 12 to 128 arXiv cs titles per year between 2023 and
2026; *Judging the Judges*, *From Yes-Men to Truth-Tellers* and *Pride and Prejudice*
each work once. Checkable: run a title-field search on the construction and count the
hits in the last twelve months.

**R10. Do not build a rhetorical question the paper then answers.** Only 4 of 54 titles
are questions, and 2 of the 4 are surveys (arXiv:2406.01297) or analyses
(arXiv:2606.23196) whose subject genuinely is the open question. For a primary
experiment reporting a result, the question form gives away a finding the paper has.

## Constructions to avoid, with the evidence they are avoided

**"Towards X".** One instance in 54 (arXiv:2310.13548, October 2023), and none after
2023 in the corpus. It marks a paper as preliminary.

**"The Illusion of X" and its relatives.** Three in the corpus, but the wider count shows
why that is misleading: 128 arXiv cs titles in eight months of 2026 against 12 in all of
2023. The frame no longer identifies a paper.

**A colon head that describes rather than names.** The corpus's colon heads are names
(*CRITIC*, *ELEPHANT*, *InfoQuest*, *Self-Refine*, *Self-Polish*) or claims (*Confidence
Matters*, *Reasoning Isn't Enough*, *Clarification Is Not Enough*, *Feedback Friction*).
None is a bare description of the paper's genre.

**An unbounded negative claim.** No title in the corpus asserts a flat impossibility. The
strongest, arXiv:2310.01798, adds *Yet*; arXiv:2311.08516 adds the positive half;
arXiv:2410.20513 restricts to *Innate*; arXiv:2402.08115 says *Limitations* rather than
*Failure*. Overclaiming in a title is the one error a referee reads before the abstract.

**"Extrinsic" as the paired term for "intrinsic".** One arXiv cs record uses the phrase
"extrinsic self-correction" (arXiv:2410.20513). The field's paired term is *external
feedback* (arXiv:2310.01798 abstract; arXiv:2506.11930 title). A reader searching this
literature will not be searching on *extrinsic*.

**Question titles for primary experimental results.** Four of 54, and the ones that work
belong to surveys and analyses.

**Word repetition inside the title.** *Automatically Correcting Large Language Models:
Surveying the landscape of diverse self-correction strategies* (arXiv:2308.03188) repeats
the correction stem twice in 13 words; *An Empirical Study of LLM-as-a-Judge for LLM
Evaluation* (arXiv:2403.02839) repeats *LLM* twice before the colon. Both are among the
longest titles in the corpus.

## Checklist

Run all ten before the title is settled.

1. Word count is 6 to 12. (R1)
2. If there is a colon, the head is 3 words or fewer and is a name or a claim. (R2)
3. If the finding is a limitation, the form is a flat declarative or a bounded colon
   claim, not a question. (R3, R10)
4. Every negation in the title has a stated bound (time, condition, or frequency), and
   the bound is one the design measures. (R4)
5. The title's operative phrase appears in the abstract at least once as a definition.
   (R5)
6. Each term either already exists in the field's title vocabulary, or is being coined
   deliberately for the paper's new construct. Search each term in the arXiv title field
   and record the hit count. (R6)
7. No *Towards*, and no hedge that the design cannot support. (R7)
8. The object of study is the grammatical subject. (R8)
9. The construction has been searched in the title field and is not saturated. (R9)
10. No stem repeats inside the title.

## How this paper's current title measures up

**Intrinsic Self-Correction Requires Extrinsic Direction**

**Word count: 6.** Inside the 6 to 12 band (R1), at its short end. Five of 54 corpus
titles run 4 to 6 words. The shortest declarative in the corpus, *LLMs Get Lost In
Multi-Turn Conversation* (arXiv:2505.06120), is also 6 words.

**Form: plain declarative, no colon.** Nine of 54 titles take this form, and 6 of those
9 report a limitation, which is what this paper reports. On the raw distribution the
choice is a minority one (the colon is 32 of 54 and rising). On the conditional
distribution it is the field's standard form for a negative finding. The comparison
class is not all 54 titles; it is the 20 that report a limitation, and within that group
declaratives run 6 of 20 against 10 colon, 2 noun phrase, 2 question. The choice is
supported.

**Subject-first (R8): yes.** *Intrinsic Self-Correction* is the object of study and the
grammatical subject, matching the pattern in arXiv:2410.20513 (*Self-correction is Not
An Innate Capability in Language Models*).

**Verb: Requires.** Absent from all 54 corpus titles. Four verified examples establish
the construction in the wider LLM literature, one of them at EMNLP (arXiv:2406.18002),
so it will read as ordinary rather than odd. It is also doing something the corpus verbs
do not: it states a dependency rather than a capability. *Cannot* and *is not* deny;
*causes*, *reduces*, *increases* assert an effect. *Requires* asserts a necessary
condition, which is a stronger logical claim than either, and the design has to support
it.

**Field-standard terms: one of two.** *Intrinsic self-correction* is established: 7
arXiv cs titles and 24 records, all 2024 or later, tracing to the definition in
arXiv:2310.01798's abstract. *Extrinsic* is not. "Extrinsic self-correction" returns one
arXiv cs record; "extrinsic direction" returns one arXiv record and it is a 2018 physics
paper on graphene detectors. The phrase this literature uses for the other half of the
pair is *external feedback* (arXiv:2310.01798, arXiv:2506.11930). This is the title's one
substantive risk under R6.

**Names a phenomenon: partly.** *Extrinsic direction* is a coined phrase, and R5 requires
the abstract to define it and reuse it. Six of six abstracts checked reuse their title
phrase, most of them four times. This is a testable condition on the abstract rather than
a defect in the title.

**Hedging: none.** No *Yet*, *When*, *Often*, or modal. Under R4 this is the second risk.
Every negative title in the corpus bounds its claim: *Yet* (arXiv:2310.01798), the
positive half in the same sentence (arXiv:2311.08516), *Innate* (arXiv:2410.20513),
*Limitations* rather than *Failure* (arXiv:2402.08115), *Often* (arXiv:2509.12517).
*Requires* with no qualifier states a universal necessary condition. Six models, 40
tasks and 720 trials establish that revision without stated direction does not improve
outputs across the conditions measured. They do not establish that no intrinsic
correction ever occurs without direction, which is what the unqualified *Requires*
asserts. Two ways to bring the title inside R4 without changing its form: qualify the
verb (*Usually Requires*, *Mostly Requires*), or qualify the object so the claim is
about revision yield rather than about correction in general.

**Unclaimed: yes.** The exact phrase "intrinsic self-correction requires" returns 0
arXiv records.

**No stem repetition, no *Towards*, no question, no saturated construction.** Passes
checklist items 7, 9 and 10.

**Summary.** The title passes on length, form, subject placement, novelty of phrasing
and construction freshness. The deliberate choice of a plain declarative over a colon is
supported by the evidence: within the 20 negative-finding titles, declaratives are the
second most common form and include the three most-cited negative results in this
literature. Two items need a decision. First, *Extrinsic* is not the field's word, and
*external feedback* is; the title trades searchability for the symmetry of
intrinsic/extrinsic, and that trade should be made knowingly. Second, *Requires* with no
bound is the only unqualified universal in a corpus where every comparable title states
one.
