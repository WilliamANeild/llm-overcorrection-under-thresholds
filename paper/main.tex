\section*{Limitations}

In Studies~1--2, we do not control or evaluate the quality of Turn~1 outputs. Study~3 addresses this by evaluating all turns with a blind judge, confirming that 93.3\% of Turn~1 outputs are already rated sufficient (level $\geq 4$).

Studies~1--2 use two probes selected from a five-probe pilot. While the pilot revealed a steep compliance cliff, five probes cannot definitively distinguish a binary cliff from a steep continuous function. A finer-grained probe gradient would better characterize the transition region.

Studies~1--2 use GPT-4o as the sole judge. Study~3 selects the evaluator empirically: Claude Sonnet~4 was chosen based on highest human-judge correlation ($r = 0.505$) from a six-model calibration. Human validation of Study~3 shows acceptable binary threshold agreement (85.9\% human-evaluator, 90.6\% between the strongest rater pair), but ordinal inter-rater reliability is low (Krippendorff's $\alpha = 0.237$), driven primarily by one rater whose scores do not correlate with either the other raters or the automated evaluator. We report this transparently; the binary sufficient/not-sufficient threshold that drives most Study~3 analyses is better validated than the full ordinal scale.

All scenarios are short-form tasks in English. Study~3 expands from 8 writing scenarios (Studies~1--2) to 40 scenarios across five domains including code and data logic, but long-form and reasoning tasks remain untested. Results are from specific model snapshots and may not hold across model updates.

Study~3 uses 3 runs per cell with temperature~1.0. The response classification (revision vs.\ meta-response) uses keyword matching rather than a trained classifier, though manual inspection of the 150 human-annotated samples (with response type labels) suggests the automated classification aligns with human judgment on the content/meta-response boundary.

Study~3's edge case framework controls for compositional artifacts, survivorship bias, and T1 stratification, but the balanced panel (18.8\% of trials) is small for some per-model analyses. Gemini's balanced panel contains only 2 trials; its per-model trajectory should be interpreted with caution.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

Studies~1--2 involve no human subjects. Study~3 includes human evaluation by three raters (the first author and two research assistants) who scored AI-generated outputs on a quality scale. This evaluation involved reading and rating synthetic text only; no personal data was collected, and raters participated voluntarily. All data is generated by commercial LLM APIs in response to synthetic scenarios spanning workplace communication, code, and data tasks. The scenarios contain no sensitive content. We do not release model outputs that could be mistaken for human-written text.
